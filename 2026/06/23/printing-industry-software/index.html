What is printing industry software?

Direct answer

Printing industry software is business software that helps print, signage and packaging companies manage the full job cycle: estimating, quoting, artwork approvals, job cards, production planning, stock, WIP, delivery, invoicing, accounting, job costing and reporting.

As a printing company, your business is different to standard manufacturing or distribution. On a normal day, you estimate a custom job, calculate materials, allow for wastage, plan machine time, manage artwork, track approvals, control production, handle finishing, manage dispatch and protect margin.

That is why standard accounting software, spreadsheets and generic quoting tools often fall short. Print does not only need admin software. It needs operational software that understands how a job is estimated, produced, costed and delivered.

Key stages in printing production

A print job moves through several important stages before it reaches the customer. Your software needs to keep those stages connected, so information does not get lost between sales, estimating, artwork, production, stores, dispatch and accounts.

1

Customer provides final artwork files

2

Artwork is checked for print readiness

3

Customer approves digital or physical proof

4

Plates are created for printing machines

5

Job is printed on selected substrate with specified inks and colours

6

Post-press processes like cutting, folding or coating are applied

7

Final product is inspected for defects

8

Job is packaged for delivery

9

Job is shipped to the customer’s specified location

Why printing companies need different software to standard businesses

Printing companies work with custom jobs. One customer may need 500 business cards. Another may need 20,000 cartons. Another may need signage, labels, retail POS material, brochures, banners or short-run digital print.

In a normal product business, the product is often already defined. The business knows the cost, the selling price and the stock position. In printing, the product is often built around the customer’s requirement. The final cost depends on how the job is estimated, planned and produced.

Printing industry software must deal with the full chain of work, from the first estimate to the final invoice.
1 Wrong estimate

If the estimate is wrong, the whole job is at risk before production even starts.

2 Wrong stock information

If stock information is wrong, production can stop at the worst possible time.

3 Unclear job card

If the job card is unclear, the wrong work can be produced or rework can increase.

4 Poor WIP visibility

If WIP is unclear, customer service spends time chasing production for updates.

5 No actual cost measurement

If actual costs are not measured, you do not know whether the job was profitable.

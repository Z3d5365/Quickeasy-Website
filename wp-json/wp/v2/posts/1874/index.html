Manufacturing companies rely on software and systems to ensure their operations are efficient, effective, and profitable. However, these systems also come with inherent risks<\/a>. These risks can lead to costly errors, downtime, and security breaches. For this reason, it’s critical for manufacturers to conduct thorough risk assessments and implement risk management strategies to protect their operations and assets. In this article, we’ll explore why software and system risk assessment and management are essential for manufacturers, the benefits of risk management, how to conduct a risk assessment, and how to mitigate risk using an ERP solution like Quickeasy BOS.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

\u201cSurviving and thriving requires keen recognition and response to change. A manufacturer\u2019s risk assessment practices should incorporate agility and flexibility, so the company can recognise and respond to risks that were not evident a year or two earlier.\u201d<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Why system risk assessment is important<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Manufacturing companies operate in a dynamic and rapidly changing environment, with new technologies emerging regularly. As such, it’s essential to assess and manage the risks associated with these technologies and systems continually. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

Fragmented technology<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

As new apps and software become available, they are gradually added and patched into the operations cycle. While each app brings its unique benefits, not many are designed to seamlessly communicate company data between itself and other apps.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

For example, using a separate accounting system to your manufacturing platform could open up the business to risk, errors, time delays, duplicate work, and confusion. Ideally, accounting, manufacturing, and back office systems should be integrated into the ERP system<\/a>.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Software and system failures can result in lost productivity, revenue, and damage to the company’s reputation. Furthermore, in today’s digital age, cybersecurity threats are a constant concern. A single cyber-attack can cause a significant impact on the business, ranging from financial losses to stolen intellectual property.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Outdated technology<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

In many instances, manufacturers have a system that was custom-built for them a few decades back when ERP systems were in their infancy<\/a>. Slow, temperamental, laborious, difficult to maintain and update, these systems prefer to work in isolation and often lack the agility needed in modern business to interface with other essential systems.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Or, even more concerning, manufacturers may use spreadsheets and other manual, non-communicative systems to run their business.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

This exposes the company to significant risk from human error, time-wasting processes, and lack of clarity.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Lack of process control<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

Sometimes the organisation has all the latest tech, integrations, and systems in place. However, processes and workflows are not set up with risk management in mind.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Without accurate setups, backups, documentation, workflow and process controls in place, the organisation could be exposed to significant risk.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

The benefits of risk management<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Effective risk management strategies can help manufacturers to identify potential risks and take measures to prevent them from occurring. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

By conducting a risk assessment, companies can develop an understanding of their risks and develop mitigation strategies. This can include regular system updates and security patches, employee training, and more. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

By implementing these strategies, manufacturers can reduce their risk exposure and minimise the impact of any potential threats. This leads to greater operational efficiency, increased productivity, and enhanced security.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

How to carry out a risk assessment<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

A risk assessment involves identifying potential threats, assessing their likelihood and impact, and developing a mitigation plan. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  1. Systems inventory<\/strong>. The first step is to identify the assets and systems that are critical to the manufacturing process, including hardware, software, and data. This involves conducting a thorough inventory of all systems, including those used by third-party vendors. <\/li>\n\n\n\n
  2. Threat analysis<\/strong>. Once the inventory is complete, the next step is to identify potential threats, such as hardware failures, software vulnerabilities, and cyber-attacks. The likelihood and impact of each threat are then assessed, and a risk score is assigned.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  3. Impact analysis<\/strong>. Once you’ve identified potential risks, the next step is to assess their likelihood and impact. If you use an ERP system like QuickEasy BOS then it includes powerful analytics tools that enable you to evaluate risks based on various scenarios. By simulating different scenarios, you can gain a better understanding of the potential impact of each risk and prioritise your risk mitigation efforts accordingly.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  4. Risk management plan<\/strong>. Once the risk assessment is complete, manufacturers can develop a mitigation plan that outlines the steps necessary to reduce or eliminate the identified risks. This includes implementing measures to prevent cyber-attacks, such as firewalls and antivirus software, regular data backups, workflow checks, employee training, and more. By regularly reviewing and updating the mitigation plan, manufacturers can stay ahead of emerging threats and reduce their risk exposure continually.<\/li>\n<\/ol>\n\n\n\n

What is ERP data migration?

ERP data migration is when agreed business information is moved from your existing systems, prepared, converted into the structure required by your new ERP, and checked that it has moved correctly.

Your starting point may be:

  • A new or upgraded ERP system for small to medium businesses
  • An accounting package
  • Estimating software
  • Printing industry software
  • Inventory management software
  • Production planning software
  • CRM for SMEs
  • Spreadsheets
  • Several disconnected systems used together

Many businesses discover during migration that their information is spread more widely than they thought. Finance may have one source. Sales has another. Production relies on spreadsheets. Procurement has supplier price lists stored separately. Before we can move your data, we first need to understand what you have and where it lives.

What should you move to your new ERP?

There is no universal migration list. What matters is what your business needs to operate properly from go-live. We normally find it useful to think about your existing data in four groups:

Move

This is the information your people rely on to do their jobs.

Depending on your business, that may include:

  • customers
  • suppliers
  • materials
  • stock items
  • cost centres
  • services
  • supplier prices
  • production machines
  • other important master data

When we convert data into BOS, we can work with information from existing estimating, accounting, or ERP systems and bring agreed data into the new environment. For operational businesses, that often goes well beyond a list of customers and suppliers.

Clean

One of the simplest rules in ERP migration is: Don’t migrate rubbish. The age-old “garbage in, garbage out” applies even to your new, upgraded ERP system. If your old system contains three versions of the same customer, obsolete material codes, inactive suppliers, incorrect contact details, or inconsistent descriptions, copying all of it into BOS gives you the same problem in a newer system.

Migration is a good opportunity to clean the information you actually want to keep.

That does not mean spending months trying to make every historical record perfect. It means identifying the data your team will use and making sure it is fit for purpose.

Reconcile

Some information needs more than a quick visual check. Financial balances, stock values, and other critical figures need to agree with the source information that has been signed off. If your old system says one thing and the new ERP says another, you need to know why before go-live. That is why balancing and sign-off form part of our cut-over process.

Archive

Some information still matters without needing to sit inside your live ERP every day. Older historical records may be useful for audit, reporting, customer queries, or reference, but loading everything accumulated over ten or fifteen years is not always the best choice.

Ask a simple question: Will someone need this information regularly inside BOS?

If the answer is yes, we can look at bringing it across. If it only needs to remain available for occasional reference, an archive may make more sense.

How do you know the migrated data is correct?

Don’t judge migration success because the import completed without an error message. The business needs to check the information too.

  • Your finance team understands what should balance.

  • Sales recognises its customers.
  • Production understands materials, machines, cost centres, and the information needed to get a job through the factory.

These people need to be involved. We also test the wider BOS environment with your team before final cut-over. 

What happens while your old system is still changing?

Eventually, you need a clear cut-off. Imagine your team is still raising orders, processing payments, changing stock, and creating transactions in the old system while final migration is happening.

You now have two moving targets. That makes reconciliation much harder.

As part of the QuickEasy cut-over process, we agree the point at which the final transactions take place in the old system. Final data is then converted and checked, balances are taken on and reconciled, and the business moves across to BOS once the agreed checks have been completed.
